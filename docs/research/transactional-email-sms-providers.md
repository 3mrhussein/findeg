# Transactional email and SMS providers for FindEg

_Research snapshot: 2026-08-30. This is a comparison, not a provider selection._

## Question and scope

This artifact answers [Research transactional email and SMS providers for FindEg](https://github.com/3mrhussein/findeg/issues/44): which providers could support reliable Arabic/English transactional email and SMS for FindEg, with enough evidence to make a later provider decision?

The current [Define the FindEg system rehabilitation blueprint](https://github.com/3mrhussein/findeg/issues/25) map has narrowed since the research ticket was written. Phase one is therefore limited here to home-delivery, cash-on-delivery (COD) notifications:

- Order acceptance and lifecycle/status events.
- Delivery coordination and recording of COD payment.
- Cancellation and refund lifecycle events where applicable.

Online payment and School Collection notifications are deferred. The delivery design and provider comparison remain applicable when those capabilities return, but this research does not assume payment-gateway or School Collection traffic in phase one.

The candidate set is intentionally representative, not an exhaustive directory. Email includes the incumbent, an operations-focused service, a low-unit-cost cloud primitive, and a combined communications platform. SMS includes an Egypt/MENA specialist plus three global platforms with documented Egypt routes.

## Findings that should shape the later decision

1. **Email and SMS are different procurement problems.** Transactional email uses a verified sender/domain ([Resend domain guide](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/introduction), [Postmark sender guide](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/user-guide/send-email-with-api)) and Internet email deliverability; the reviewed email services do not publish an Egypt-specific delivery guarantee. SMS depends on an Egypt route, operator behavior, registered sender identity, and per-segment economics ([Twilio Egypt guideline](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/eg/sms)).
2. **Arabic materially changes SMS cost and payload size.** Arabic uses UCS-2: 70 characters in one SMS or 67 characters per part in a concatenated SMS, versus GSM-7's 160/153. One non-GSM character changes the whole message to UCS-2, and providers charge each segment separately ([Twilio character limits](https://www.twilio.com/docs/glossary/what-sms-character-limit), [Infobip multilingual SMS](https://www.infobip.com/docs/sms/sms-multilingual)). English is not automatically GSM-7: smart quotes, emoji, or other unsupported characters also trigger UCS-2.
3. **Egypt sender onboarding is on the critical path.** Infobip restricts local sender IDs to customers contracting with Infobip Egypt and asks for commercial registration, tax card, and a delegation letter; its sender name is limited to 11 characters ([Egypt LOA guide](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/mena-registration/egypt-letter-of-authorization-loa-guidelines)). Twilio estimates about three weeks for a domestic alphanumeric sender and documents operator filtering ([Egypt SMS guideline](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/eg/sms)). CEQUENS asks for local commercial registration, tax card, and an NOC/letter and registers across Egypt's four mobile networks ([sender-registration guide](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002303448-how-can-i-register-a-sender-id-)). AWS also requires registration, an LOA, company/tax documents, opt-in details, volumes, and sample messages ([AWS Egypt registration](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/registrations-egypt.html)). These are provider workflows, not a complete statement of Egyptian law; legal/compliance review remains necessary.
4. **“Accepted” is not “delivered.”** The application must retain the provider message ID and reconcile asynchronous outcomes. Even then, carrier receipts can be incomplete or delayed: CEQUENS says operators do not always return a DLR ([status definitions](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002294135-what-does-each-sms-status-mean-)); Twilio warns that domestic Egypt DLRs may be delayed and concatenated-message DLRs cover only the first segment ([Egypt SMS guideline](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/eg/sms)); AWS says a delivery receipt can take up to 72 hours and can then become `UNKNOWN` ([event destinations](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/configuration-sets-event-destinations.html)).
5. **Public SMS prices are not directly comparable.** Twilio publishes an Egypt per-segment price. Infobip, CEQUENS, and AWS route pricing is calculator-, account-, or quote-dependent. A later decision needs like-for-like quotes for the same registered sender type, operator mix, expected monthly segments, and transactional route.

## Current implementation evidence

FindEg currently depends on Resend `^4.0.2` ([backend/package.json](../../backend/package.json)) and sends React Email templates through [`ResendEmailService`](../../backend/src/features/notifications/infrastructure/ResendEmailService.ts). Existing templates cover password reset, Order confirmation/status, admin invitation, and school-list access. The service awaits `emails.send` but catches the error and returns; it does not retain the provider message ID, supply an idempotency key, or consume delivery webhooks. There is no SMS adapter.

That makes Resend the lowest-migration email candidate, not a predetermined choice. Whichever providers are selected, the reliability gap is primarily in FindEg's delivery workflow rather than its template syntax.

## Email candidates

Email Arabic/English content should be generated as UTF-8 HTML plus plain text and visually tested in right-to-left and left-to-right clients. Unlike SMS, email has no per-character segment charge. Provider acceptance still does not prove inbox placement in Egypt; test the actual customer mailbox mix and authenticate the sender domain.

### Resend — incumbent, developer-oriented email API

- **Language, sender, and Egypt:** The send API accepts HTML, text, or React content and returns an email ID ([send API](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/emails/send-email)). Sending beyond the account owner's test address requires a verified domain; Resend's domain setup uses SPF and DKIM, with DMARC recommended ([domain guide](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/introduction)). There is no published Egypt-specific inbox-delivery commitment, so Arabic rendering and local mailbox placement need empirical tests.
- **API and observability:** Webhook events include sent, delivered, delayed, bounced, failed, complained, suppressed, opened, and clicked ([event types](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/event-types)). The dashboard exposes message preview, headers, events, and logs ([email dashboard](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/introduction)).
- **Retries and idempotency:** `Idempotency-Key` deduplicates an identical send for 24 hours ([idempotency guide](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys)). Webhook delivery is at least once, may arrive out of order, carries `svix-id` for deduplication, and retries after 5 seconds, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours, 5 hours, and 10 hours ([webhook guide](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/introduction)). Webhook creation returns a signing secret ([create-webhook API](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/webhooks/create-webhook)).
- **Test mode:** The shared `resend.dev` domain can send only to the email address associated with the account; other recipients require a verified domain ([testing restriction](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/403-error-resend-dev-domain)). Resend does not require a separate production-access review once the domain is verified ([production access](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/does-resend-require-production-approval)).
- **Security and data:** Resend documents SOC 2 Type II, GDPR support, encryption at rest, TLS 1.3+, signed webhooks, and a DPA ([security](https://resend.com/docs/security), [DPA](https://resend.com/legal/dpa)). Sending regions include Northern Virginia, Ireland, São Paulo, and Tokyo, but account metadata, logs, and API records are stored in the United States regardless of sending region ([regions](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/regions)).
- **Price, support, and integration burden:** The public plans list Free at $0 for 3,000 emails/month with a 100/day cap; Pro at $20/month for 50,000 plus $0.90/1,000 overage; and Scale at $90/month for 100,000. Ticket support is listed on all plans and Slack support on Scale ([pricing](https://resend.com/pricing)). **Inferred FindEg burden: low for basic sending, medium for reliable delivery** because templates and SDK already exist, while the outbox, saved message ID, signed webhook consumer, and error handling do not.

### Postmark — transactional-email operations focus

- **Language, sender, and Egypt:** The API accepts HTML and text bodies and returns `MessageID`; sending requires a confirmed sender signature or domain ([email API](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/api/email-api), [sending guide](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/user-guide/send-email-with-api)). It has no published Egypt-specific inbox-delivery guarantee; validate Arabic rendering and representative Egyptian mailbox providers.
- **API and observability:** Message history and details expose delivery events and message metadata; default message retention is 45 days and can be configured from 7 to 365 days with its retention option ([messages API](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/api/messages-api)). Webhooks cover delivery, bounce, open, click, spam complaint, and subscription changes ([webhook overview](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/webhooks/webhooks-overview)).
- **Retries and idempotency:** Postmark retries webhook failures on a documented sequence for network failures, `408`, `429`, and `5xx`, and recommends deduplication using `MessageID` plus `X-PM-Webhook-Trace-Id`; it does not cryptographically sign webhooks, instead documenting HTTPS, basic authentication in the URL, and IP allowlisting ([webhook overview](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/webhooks/webhooks-overview)). Postmark explicitly says it has no email-send idempotency key ([idempotency answer](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/what-is-an-idempotency-key)); FindEg would need durable local send deduplication.
- **Test mode:** Sandbox servers black-hole messages but expose simulated `Delivered` results through the UI, API, and webhooks; these messages count toward the plan ([sandbox mode](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/user-guide/sandbox-mode/server-sandbox-mode)). The `POSTMARK_API_TEST` token validates API requests without delivery ([sending guide](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/user-guide/send-email-with-api)). New accounts initially send only to their own confirmed address until Postmark approves sending ([getting started](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/get-started)).
- **Security and data:** Postmark says its primary systems are hosted in a data center outside Chicago and AWS; messages are not encrypted at rest because the UI and authorized support access require readable content. Sent content is retained for 45 days by default, while bounce/complaint suppressions are retained indefinitely ([security and redundancy](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/is-postmark-secure-and-redundant)). Postmark offers a DPA and GDPR materials ([GDPR guide](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/1168-postmarks-gdpr-compliance)) but states that Postmark itself has not undergone a SOC audit; its infrastructure providers have certifications ([EU privacy FAQ](https://postmarkapp.com/eu-privacy)).
- **Price, support, and integration burden:** The Free plan includes 100 emails/month for testing. Basic starts at $15/month for 10,000 emails, $1.80/1,000 overage, five domains, unlimited daily sends, and 45-day retention ([pricing](https://postmarkapp.com/pricing/)). Support is email-based; Postmark says typical replies arrive within a few business hours and it does not offer phone or Zoom support ([support introduction](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/welcome-to-postmark-getting-started-for-new-customers)). **Inferred burden: medium-low**: good sandbox/operations tooling, but a new SDK/template adapter and a local outbox are required.

### Amazon SES — granular AWS email primitive

- **Language, sender, and Egypt:** `SendEmail` supports simple, raw, and templated content and allows UTF-8 for subject, text, and HTML character sets ([API/CLI reference](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cli/latest/reference/sesv2/send-email.html)). A production sender must verify an address/domain ([verified identities](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/creating-identities.html)); there is no published Egypt-specific inbox-delivery guarantee.
- **API and observability:** SES can publish send, delivery, delayed-delivery, bounce, complaint, reject, open, click, rendering-failure, and subscription events through configuration sets to services such as SNS, CloudWatch, or Firehose ([monitoring guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/monitor-sending-activity.html)). SES returns a message ID; AWS recommends storing a mapping from the application's ID to the SES ID because custom IDs are not retained in SES notifications ([notification troubleshooting](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/troubleshoot-notifications.html)).
- **Retries and idempotency:** SES retries soft bounces for a period but does not publish the exact retry duration ([success metrics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/success-metrics.html)). The documented `SendEmail` request has no client idempotency token ([SendEmail API](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/APIReference-V2/API_SendEmail.html)); treat local outbox/deduplication as mandatory.
- **Test mode:** A new account is sandboxed per AWS Region, may send only to verified recipients or the mailbox simulator, and is limited to 200 messages per 24 hours and one message per second until production access is approved ([production-access guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/request-production-access.html)).
- **Security and data:** IAM policies can limit send actions and sender/recipient/return-path identities ([authorization guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/control-user-access.html)); CloudTrail records SES API activity ([CloudTrail guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/logging-using-cloudtrail.html)). AWS documents TLS controls, encryption, and the shared-responsibility model for SES ([data protection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/data-protection.html)); SES and SNS are within AWS's SOC scope ([services in scope](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/services-in-scope/SOC/)). Region choice, SNS/event payloads, logs, backups, and retention still need an explicit data-flow review.
- **Price, support, and integration burden:** Outbound email is $0.10 per 1,000 recipients plus $0.12/GB for attachment data, with separate charges for related AWS services where applicable ([SES pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/pricing/)). AWS support is a separate plan with current tiers and response targets ([AWS Support](https://aws.amazon.com/premiumsupport/plans/)). **Inferred burden: highest of the email set** unless FindEg already standardizes on AWS: domain/DKIM, production approval, IAM, configuration sets, SNS/CloudWatch/queue consumers, and an operational UI all need setup.

### Infobip Email — email within a multi-channel CPaaS

- **Language, sender, and Egypt:** Infobip supports transactional email over HTTP API or SMTP after domain verification, with HTML/text payloads and delivery/engagement tracking ([Email over API](https://www.infobip.com/docs/email/email-over-api)). No Egypt-specific inbox-delivery guarantee is published; Arabic/RTL rendering and local mailbox placement require testing.
- **API and observability:** Email delivery reports can be pushed to a webhook or polled, but a polled report is returned only once ([email delivery reports](https://www.infobip.com/docs/email/email-over-api/email-api-delivery-reports)). Cross-channel reporting webhooks support Basic authentication, HMAC, OAuth 2.0, or mutual TLS; Infobip retries a failed webhook up to 20 times over 41 hours 30 minutes, after which the event is lost, and its logs/polling fallback covers the last 48 hours ([reporting webhooks](https://www.infobip.com/docs/reporting/webhooks)).
- **Retries and idempotency:** Public email documentation does not identify a send idempotency key. Use a durable local operation ID and outbox; do not confuse Infobip's documented custom-ID duplicate rejection for the SMS endpoint with email behavior.
- **Test mode:** The 60-day free trial restricts traffic to verified phone numbers/email addresses and uses predefined test senders; channel allowances vary ([free trial](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/getting-started/free-trial)). Validate whether the trial exercises the same email route and event configuration intended for production.
- **Security and data:** Infobip documents 2FA, API-key scopes, IP safelisting, and TLS 1.2 ([security recommendations](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/manage-my-account/security-recommendations), [API authorization](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/api-essentials/api-authorization)). Its security terms require its security program to maintain specified ISO certifications and a SOC 2 Type I report or equivalent ([information-security terms](https://www.infobip.com/policies/information-security-terms)). Its infrastructure guide describes only particular local SaaS regions and says non-SaaS reporting data is replicated to Frankfurt; it does not document Egypt-local residency ([infrastructure overview](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/support/infrastructure-overview)). Confirm the contracted processing location, retention, DPA, and subprocessors.
- **Price, support, and integration burden:** Infobip publishes a usage-based calculator but the accessible public page does not expose a stable Egypt-email rate; obtain an account-specific quote ([pricing](https://www.infobip.com/pricing)). Support is available through the account and support email, with retention/support details in its handbook ([support handbook](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/support/support-handbook)). **Inferred burden: medium**: one account and reporting plane can cover both channels, offset by enterprise-style onboarding, quote dependence, and cross-channel webhook/polling semantics.

### Email comparison snapshot

| Candidate | Distinguishing strength | Main decision caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resend | Reuses FindEg's SDK and React Email templates; documented 24-hour send idempotency and signed webhooks | United States metadata/log storage; current FindEg adapter omits the reliability features |
| Postmark | Strong sandbox and transactional-message operations UX | No send idempotency key or webhook signature; US-hosted readable message content; service itself lacks a SOC audit |

## SMS candidates

### Shared message and sender implications

- Author bilingual templates as separate, versioned Arabic and English messages; calculate the encoded segment count before sending. Set concise maximum lengths per event, strip accidental emoji/smart punctuation when GSM-7 English is intended, and record encoding plus segment count for cost analysis.
- An alphanumeric sender ID is generally brand-like and one-way. A long/short number is needed where two-way replies are a real product requirement, but Egypt number capability varies by provider: Twilio reports no two-way SMS for Egypt ([Twilio Egypt guideline](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/eg/sms)), while AWS lists two-way and short-code support but no long codes ([AWS country capabilities](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/phone-numbers-sms-by-country.html)). This is a provider-route difference, not a contradiction that can be generalized to all vendors.
- Phase-one COD lifecycle notices appear one-way, so a registered brand sender is the primary comparison. If delivery coordination needs replies, price and validate a two-way number/workflow separately rather than assuming the notification route supports it.

### CEQUENS — Egypt/MENA-oriented SMS provider

- **Egypt coverage and sender:** CEQUENS has a Cairo office ([contact page](https://www.cequens.com/contact)) and publishes an Egypt sender-registration process for Etisalat, Orange, Vodafone, and Telecom Egypt. It requests commercial registration, tax card, and an NOC/letter; OTP content on local routes must contain the brand ([registration guide](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002303448-how-can-i-register-a-sender-id-), [NOC guide](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002303456-why-do-i-need-to-request-a-no-objection-certificate-noc-)). Its published registration estimate is four business days for Vodafone and 7–10 for the other local networks, explicitly subject to change ([registration SLA](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002303453-what-is-the-expected-sla-for-each-country-)).
- **Arabic and sender behavior:** CEQUENS documents UCS-2 at 70/67 characters and says each segment consumes a credit ([encoding guide](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002783067-sms-character-limits-and-encoding-)).
- **API and observability:** `POST /api/sms/v1/messages` uses bearer-token authentication, accepts an approved sender and E.164 recipient, and returns an accepted status with `message_id` ([send guide](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002294164-how-can-i-send-a-message-using-api-)). Message details can be polled by that ID and include route, carrier, and DLR status ([message-details guide](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002294165-how-can-i-view-message-details-via-api-)).
- **Retries, idempotency, test mode, and security:** In the reviewed public documentation, no transactional-SMS send idempotency key, delivery-webhook signing/retry contract, or production-equivalent bulk-SMS sandbox was found. CEQUENS's free Verify trial is for its separate OTP verification product ([Verify trial](https://support.cequens.com/support/solutions/articles/44002294119-getting-started-with-verify)). Treat all four items as pre-contract proof-of-concept gates, not as claims that the capabilities are absent.
- **Price, support, data, and burden:** CEQUENS says SMS is usage-based/tiered with no minimum, subscription, or setup fee, but requires a custom quote; the same page states a 99.96% platform uptime SLA and lists ISO 27001, PCI DSS, and GDPR ([pricing](https://www.cequens.com/pricing)). Confirm certification scope, DPA, subprocessors, message retention/location, support response targets, Egypt-route fees, and failed-message treatment in writing. **Inferred burden: medium**: local footprint and onboarding knowledge are useful, while less-complete public API/operations evidence shifts validation into sales and a POC.

### Infobip SMS — global CPaaS with an Egyptian local-sender path

- **Egypt coverage and sender:** Only customers with a direct master service agreement with Infobip Egypt are eligible for local sender IDs; local registration asks for commercial registration, tax card, and a delegation letter. The sender is up to 11 characters and generic/special-character names are prohibited ([Egypt LOA guide](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/mena-registration/egypt-letter-of-authorization-loa-guidelines)). Infobip says international traffic does not require sender registration, but local versus international route performance and commercial terms must be quoted.
- **Arabic and segmentation:** Infobip explicitly identifies Arabic as UCS-2, 70/67 characters, and bills every segment separately ([multilingual SMS](https://www.infobip.com/docs/sms/sms-multilingual)).
- **API and observability:** The send API returns `bulkId` and `messageId`, can accept a client-supplied message ID, and supports webhook or one-time polling for results ([SMS tutorial](https://www.infobip.com/docs/tutorials/send-your-first-sms-message-using-infobip-api)). Delivery reports include status, error, timing, network, price, and custom data; webhook retries and the 48-hour polling fallback use the shared reporting behavior ([message delivery](https://www.infobip.com/docs/sms/message-delivery), [reporting webhooks](https://www.infobip.com/docs/reporting/webhooks)).
- **Retries and idempotency:** Infobip documents `REJECTED_DUPLICATE_MESSAGE_ID` for a repeated custom message ID and marks system errors as retryable ([status/error catalog](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/api-essentials/response-status-and-error-codes)). Use a stable FindEg operation ID as the provider message ID and still persist each attempt/outcome.
- **Test mode and security/data:** The SMS quickstart advertises a trial allowance and verified-number restrictions ([SMS quickstart](https://www.infobip.com/docs/sms/get-started)); the broader free-trial guide specifies 60 days, verified recipients, and predefined senders ([free trial](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/getting-started/free-trial)). Security and data-location caveats are the same as Infobip Email above.
- **Price, support, and burden:** Egypt coverage and price are account-specific; Infobip directs customers to its coverage/account manager and pricing calculator ([coverage](https://www.infobip.com/docs/sms/sms-coverage-and-connectivity), [pricing](https://www.infobip.com/pricing)). Support uses the account/support-email channels ([support handbook](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/support/support-handbook)). **Inferred burden: medium**: broad, well-documented APIs and one multi-channel account, with local-MSA onboarding and quote/contract work.

### Twilio Programmable Messaging — documented international route and public price

- **Egypt coverage and sender:** Twilio lists Egypt SMS support with concatenation but no two-way SMS. It recommends preregistered alphanumeric senders, estimates about three weeks for domestic registration and one week for international preregistration, and warns that operators filter traffic. Domestic long/short numbers are not supported; international long codes are supported but Etisalat may replace the sender with a generic alpha ID ([Egypt guideline](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/eg/sms)).
- **Arabic and segmentation:** Twilio supports UCS-2 and documents 70/67-character limits versus GSM-7's 160/153, with per-segment billing ([character limits](https://www.twilio.com/docs/glossary/what-sms-character-limit)). Its Egypt page lists UCS-2 for international/dynamic traffic; validate the final registered route and sample Arabic copy during onboarding ([Egypt guideline](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/eg/sms)).
- **API and observability:** The Messages API returns a Message SID and lifecycle status and can POST status changes/error codes to `StatusCallback` ([Message resource](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/api/message-resource)). Messaging logs expose queue/carrier steps, segment count, errors, and status ([Messaging Logs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/features/messaging-logs)). Webhooks carry `X-Twilio-Signature`, which Twilio SDKs can validate ([webhook security](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/webhooks/webhooks-security)).
- **Retries and idempotency:** Callback connection overrides support zero to five connection retries and include `I-Twilio-Idempotency-Token` on retry attempts ([webhook connection overrides](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/webhooks/webhooks-connection-overrides)). That token deduplicates inbound callback attempts; the documented outbound Message creation request has no send idempotency key. Twilio's duplicate-message guide points to repeated application POSTs as the usual cause ([debugging guide](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/guides/debugging-common-issues)), so use a local outbox/send key.
- **Test mode:** Test credentials simulate SMS API responses without sending, charging, or creating real resources, but do not invoke status callbacks ([test credentials](https://www.twilio.com/docs/iam/test-credentials)). A trial account lasts 30 days and restricts destinations/content ([trial guide](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/tutorials/how-to-use-your-free-trial-account)). A real low-volume Egypt-route test is still required.
- **Price, support, security/data, and burden:** The public Egypt rate is $0.3959 per outbound segment for an international number or alphanumeric sender; Twilio also lists a $0.001 fee for messages ending in a failed status and says carrier fees may apply. Domestic pricing/discounts require sales contact ([Egypt pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/eg)). Free Developer support has no guaranteed response time; paid plans begin with Production at the greater of $250/month or 4% of monthly spend, with 24/7 coverage ([support plans](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/support-plans)). Twilio provides API-key guidance, signed callbacks, and a DPA ([API security practices](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/rest-api-best-practices), [DPA](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/legal/data-protection-addendum)); confirm processing region/retention for the Egypt route. **Inferred burden: low-medium** technically, with unusually transparent but high public per-segment economics and sender-registration lead time.

### AWS End User Messaging SMS — AWS-native messaging primitive

- **Egypt coverage and sender:** AWS lists short codes and two-way SMS in Egypt, no long codes, required sender-ID registration, and international sending ([country capabilities](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/phone-numbers-sms-by-country.html)). Its Egypt registration requires a 3–11-character alphanumeric sender, LOA, company registration/tax documents for a local business, opt-in workflow, expected volume, and sample messages; the use case must be transactional ([Egypt registration](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/registrations-egypt.html)). Confirm which origination identity and two-way route are commercially available for FindEg rather than inferring that every listed capability can be combined.
- **Arabic and segmentation:** AWS's send API accepts text content, while pricing is per message part; validate the SDK's encoding/segment calculation and final Egypt route with Arabic fixtures before comparing quotes. The general UCS-2 70/67 rule above still governs carrier SMS payloads.
- **API and observability:** `SendTextMessage` sends to one destination, returns `MessageId`, supports `TRANSACTIONAL`, allows a 5–259,200-second time-to-live, and has `DryRun` validation without sending or charge ([SendTextMessage API](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/pinpoint/latest/apireference_smsvoicev2/API_SendTextMessage.html)). Configuration sets publish events to CloudWatch Logs, Firehose, or SNS ([event destinations](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/configuration-sets-event-destinations.html)). CloudWatch metrics persist for 15 months; AWS warns that duplicate delivery receipts can occur and says to deduplicate with the original message ID ([CloudWatch monitoring](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/monitoring-cloudwatch.html)).
- **Retries and idempotency:** The documented send request has no client idempotency token. Use a local outbox. Delivery-receipt handling must accept duplicates and the documented delayed/`UNKNOWN` outcome.
- **Test mode:** The per-Region SMS sandbox starts with a $1 monthly spend limit and allows up to 10 verified destinations; production access is requested per Region ([SMS sandbox](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/sandbox.html)). AWS also supplies simulator numbers for success/error cases ([verified/simulator numbers](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/verify-destination-phone-number.html)).
- **Price, support, security/data, and burden:** AWS exposes SMS pricing through a dynamic country/operator table and downloadable pricing data rather than a stable Egypt figure on the accessible page; obtain the current Egypt transactional-route price and registration/origination fees at decision time ([pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/end-user-messaging/pricing/)). IAM governs actions/resources and CloudTrail records API activity ([permissions](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/permissions-actions.html), [CloudTrail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/logging-using-cloudtrail.html)); support is a separate AWS plan ([AWS Support](https://aws.amazon.com/premiumsupport/plans/)). **Inferred burden: high** unless AWS operations are already standard: registration, IAM, configuration sets, SNS/CloudWatch/queue consumers, spend controls, and an operations UI all need assembly.

## Combined platform versus best-of-breed

| Stack shape | What it buys | What it costs / what to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Infobip Email + SMS** | One supplier, account, authentication model, and reporting/webhook family; documented Egypt local-sender path | Direct Egypt MSA, quote-driven economics, shared-provider concentration, and contractual data-location/retention validation |
| **Amazon SES + AWS End User Messaging SMS** | One cloud/IAM/billing environment and composable SNS/CloudWatch event paths | They remain separate products with separate sandboxes/production reviews and substantial infrastructure/operations work; SMS price remains route-specific |
| **Current Resend + a specialist SMS provider** | Preserves React Email and minimizes email migration while choosing SMS on Egypt evidence | Two vendors, contracts, credential sets, webhook models, support paths, and operational dashboards; FindEg needs a provider-neutral notification ledger |
| **Postmark or SES + an SMS provider** | Allows email operations or unit economics to be optimized independently of Egypt SMS routing | Migration plus the same two-vendor complexity; no reviewed provider removes the need for FindEg-side idempotency/reconciliation |

No stack shape is inherently the winner. A combined vendor reduces vendor surface but does not create exactly-once delivery or remove channel-specific onboarding. Best-of-breed can preserve the incumbent and optimize Egypt routing, but makes a normalized internal delivery model more important.

## Provider-neutral delivery controls (research-derived design implications)

These are inferences from the documented failure and event semantics above, not provider claims:

1. Write a notification intent to a transactional outbox with the business state change. Use a unique operation key such as `(domain event, notification kind, channel, recipient, locale)` so a retried Order command cannot create a second customer message.
2. Persist attempt number, provider, provider message ID, encoding/segment count for SMS, accepted time, last provider status, and terminal time. Keep the business event ID in provider metadata/custom data where supported.
3. Use the provider's send-idempotency feature where one exists, but keep the local unique key: Resend's protection expires after 24 hours; Postmark, SES, Twilio, and the reviewed AWS SMS send request do not document one; Infobip's custom SMS ID is the strongest documented SMS-side duplicate control in this set.
4. Verify webhook authenticity, deduplicate provider events, allow out-of-order updates, and make handlers retry-safe. Poll/reconcile where documented, but respect one-time/limited retention behavior.
5. Retry only from durable state. Distinguish a known rejection from an ambiguous network timeout: an immediate resend after an unknown outcome can duplicate a customer notification.
6. Define terminal states (`delivered`, `permanent failure`, `expired/unknown`) and escalation rules. Do not block the Order transaction on a remote provider, but do not swallow notification failure; expose it to operations and a bounded retry policy.
7. Minimize personal data in payloads, logs, provider metadata, and retained message bodies. Make retention and deletion settings part of vendor configuration and the data-processing review.
8. Keep channel policy outside adapters: event criticality, email/SMS choice, fallback, locale, quiet hours if later required, and whether a second channel is allowed after an uncertain first-channel result.
